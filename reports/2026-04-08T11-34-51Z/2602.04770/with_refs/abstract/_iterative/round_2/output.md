# Abstract

Current generative models face a fundamental trade-off between sample quality and computational efficiency, requiring multiple network evaluations during inference to progressively transform noise into data. While discriminative models simply map samples to labels, generative models must learn complex distribution mappings—a significantly more challenging task that existing methods address through iterative refinement at inference time.

We propose **Equilibrium Flow Networks (EFNs)**, a novel generative modeling paradigm that leverages the iterative nature of training itself rather than inference. Our approach defines a dynamic equilibrium condition where the model stops improving when generated samples become indistinguishable from real data according to a learned critic. During training, we alternate between updating a generator network that maps noise to data in a single forward pass, and a critic network that distinguishes between real and generated samples. The key insight is that we use the critic's feedback to create a self-correcting training signal: the generator learns to produce samples that fool an increasingly sophisticated critic, while the critic learns to detect subtle distributional mismatches.

Unlike adversarial training which can suffer from instability, our equilibrium condition provides a principled stopping criterion based on distributional convergence. The method naturally handles conditional generation by incorporating conditioning information into both generator and critic networks. We demonstrate that this approach can generate high-quality samples across multiple modalities without requiring pre-trained models or iterative inference procedures.

Our main contributions include: (1) a theoretically grounded equilibrium framework that transforms iterative inference into iterative training, (2) a stable training algorithm with convergence guarantees, and (3) empirical validation showing competitive sample quality with significantly reduced inference cost compared to diffusion-based methods.
