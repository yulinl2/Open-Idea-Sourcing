# Flow Matching: Direct Distribution Alignment Through Sample Dynamics

## Abstract

Current generative modeling approaches face a fundamental trade-off between sample quality and computational efficiency, often requiring iterative refinement procedures that perform multiple network evaluations to transform noise into data. We propose **Flow Matching**, a novel generative modeling paradigm that learns to generate high-quality samples in a single forward pass by directly aligning distributions through sample dynamics during training. Our approach formulates generation as a continuous-time flow problem where samples from the data distribution and noise distribution are connected via optimal transport paths. Rather than relying on adversarial training or variational objectives, Flow Matching defines a vector field that describes how samples should move through space to transform one distribution into another. The key insight is to leverage the iterative nature of neural network training itself: during each training step, we compute instantaneous velocities for both real and generated samples based on their spatial relationships, creating a dynamic system where samples are attracted to regions of high data density and repelled from low-density regions. This direct sample-to-sample interaction eliminates the need for discriminative components while ensuring the learned flow reaches equilibrium when distributions are perfectly aligned. The training objective minimizes the difference between predicted and target velocity fields, where target velocities are derived from optimal transport theory. Our method supports flexible conditional generation by incorporating conditioning information directly into the flow dynamics, scales effectively to high-resolution data, and avoids mode collapse through the continuous nature of the flow field. Flow Matching provides a principled framework that unifies the benefits of normalizing flows and diffusion models while requiring only a single forward pass at inference time, making it both theoretically grounded and computationally efficient for practical applications across diverse data modalities.
