# Conformal Prediction for Conditional Average Treatment Effects Under Covariate Shift

## Abstract

Understanding treatment effect heterogeneity is crucial for personalized decision-making, yet existing methods for estimating conditional average treatment effects (CATE) provide limited uncertainty quantification, particularly when the study population differs from the target population of interest. We propose **Causal Conformal Prediction**, a distribution-free framework that constructs valid prediction intervals for individual-level treatment effects without relying on asymptotic approximations or strong modeling assumptions. Our approach extends conformal prediction methodology to the causal inference setting by defining nonconformity scores based on cross-fitted pseudo-outcomes that isolate treatment effect signals from nuisance parameter estimation bias. For settings with covariate shift between study and target populations, we develop a weighted variant that incorporates likelihood ratios to maintain coverage guarantees. The method accommodates both randomized experiments and observational studies under unconfoundedness, requiring only that either the propensity score or outcome regression models are well-specified. Our framework provides finite-sample coverage guarantees that hold regardless of the complexity of the underlying machine learning algorithms used for nuisance parameter estimation. In contrast to existing approaches that rely on asymptotic normality assumptions, our intervals maintain their validity even when treatment effects are estimated using black-box methods like random forests or neural networks. The resulting prediction intervals enable practitioners to quantify uncertainty about individual treatment recommendations while accounting for potential differences between experimental and deployment populations. This addresses a critical gap in translating causal machine learning research into reliable decision-support tools for high-stakes applications in medicine, policy, and social interventions.
