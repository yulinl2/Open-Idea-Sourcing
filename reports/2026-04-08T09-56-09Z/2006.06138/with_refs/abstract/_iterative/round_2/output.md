# Conformal Prediction Intervals for Individual Treatment Effects

Estimating heterogeneous treatment effects is crucial across medicine, policy, and social sciences, where treatments may benefit some individuals while harming others. While machine learning methods have enabled flexible estimation of conditional average treatment effects (CATE), reliable uncertainty quantification for individual-level causal effects remains challenging. Existing approaches often fail to provide valid coverage guarantees, particularly when study populations differ from target populations—a common scenario in practice.

We propose a novel framework that decomposes individual treatment effect interval construction into separate conformal prediction problems for each potential outcome. For treated units, we observe Y(1) and construct prediction intervals for the unobserved Y(0); for control units, we observe Y(0) and construct intervals for Y(1). We then combine these intervals to form treatment effect bounds. Our approach leverages recent advances in weighted conformal prediction to handle covariate shift between experimental and target populations, enabling valid inference when deployment conditions differ from study settings.

The key insight is treating unobserved potential outcomes as standard prediction problems while exploiting the causal structure. Under randomized treatment assignment, we show our intervals achieve finite-sample coverage guarantees without asymptotic approximations or strong modeling assumptions. For observational studies, coverage holds under standard unconfoundedness assumptions. When likelihood ratios between study and target populations are known or estimable, our weighted extension maintains validity under covariate shift.

Our framework provides distribution-free uncertainty quantification for heterogeneous treatment effects, accommodating model misspecification while leveraging observed potential outcomes. This enables principled decision-making in sensitive applications where understanding both the magnitude and uncertainty of individual treatment effects is essential for optimal treatment allocation and policy design.
