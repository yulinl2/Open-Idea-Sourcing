# Doubly Robust Conformal Prediction for Individual Treatment Effects

## Abstract

Estimating individual treatment effects with reliable uncertainty quantification is crucial for personalized decision-making in medicine, policy, and social sciences. While machine learning methods can flexibly estimate conditional average treatment effects (CATE), they typically fail to provide valid uncertainty intervals, especially in finite samples. We propose a novel approach that combines the robustness properties of doubly robust estimation with the finite-sample validity guarantees of conformal prediction to construct prediction intervals for individual treatment effects. Our method leverages the key insight that individual treatment effect estimation can be decomposed into separate prediction problems for each potential outcome, then appropriately combined. For each unit, we observe one potential outcome directly and treat the unobserved counterfactual as a missing value prediction problem. We develop weighted conformal prediction intervals for the unobserved potential outcomes, where weights are derived from estimated propensity scores to handle both randomized experiments and observational studies. The method achieves double robustness by constructing separate conformal intervals using outcome regression models and inverse propensity weighting, then taking their intersection. This ensures valid coverage when either the outcome model or the propensity score model is correctly specified, while maintaining finite-sample validity without asymptotic approximations. Our approach naturally handles covariate shift between study and target populations through propensity score reweighting, and extends to settings where treatment assignment mechanisms differ between populations. The resulting intervals provide principled uncertainty quantification for individual treatment effects with coverage guarantees that remain valid even under model misspecification, addressing a critical gap in personalized causal inference.
