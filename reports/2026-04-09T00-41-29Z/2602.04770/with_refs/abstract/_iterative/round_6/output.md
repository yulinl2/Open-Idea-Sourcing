# Flow Matching: Single-Step Generative Modeling via Discrete Sample Transport

## Abstract

Current generative modeling approaches face a fundamental trade-off between sample quality and computational efficiency, with most high-quality methods requiring iterative refinement during inference. We propose **Flow Matching**, a novel training paradigm that learns to generate high-quality samples in a single forward pass by directly optimizing sample transport between distributions. Our approach leverages the iterative nature of training itself: at each training step, we compute explicit movement vectors for generated samples based on their pairwise interactions with real data samples through kernel-weighted similarity measures. This creates a discrete transport process where samples are attracted to high-density regions of the target distribution and repelled from low-density areas, without requiring adversarial training or discriminative signals. The key insight is to formalize sample movement as a vector field that depends on both the current generated distribution and the target distribution, while preventing direct backpropagation through distributional dependencies by treating movement computations as fixed targets. During training, generated samples evolve through discrete updates that progressively align the output distribution with the data distribution, establishing an equilibrium condition where movement naturally ceases when distributions match. Our method connects to classical non-parametric density estimation while scaling effectively to high-resolution data across multiple modalities. Flow Matching achieves competitive sample quality in a single forward pass, avoiding mode collapse through its inherent sample diversity mechanism, and supports flexible conditional generation. The approach provides a principled alternative to iterative generative models by transforming the complex inference-time computation into a learnable transport process embedded within the training dynamics.
