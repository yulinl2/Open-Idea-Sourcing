# Flow Matching for Single-Step Generative Modeling

## Abstract

Current generative models face a fundamental trade-off between sample quality and computational efficiency, with many approaches requiring iterative refinement procedures that perform multiple network evaluations at inference time. We propose **Flow Matching**, a novel training paradigm that learns to generate high-quality samples in a single forward pass by leveraging the iterative nature of training itself rather than inference. Our approach formulates generative modeling as learning a vector field that governs how samples move from a simple prior distribution toward the target data distribution through discrete training updates. Specifically, we define pairwise interaction forces between generated and real samples based on kernel similarity measures, where each training iteration computes explicit movement vectors that attract generated samples toward data regions while repelling them from low-density areas. The movement is inherently anti-symmetric—swapping data and generated distributions reverses the field direction—and naturally reaches equilibrium when the distributions align. Unlike adversarial methods, Flow Matching requires no discriminative components and instead optimizes a direct objective where the network learns to transform its input distribution by predicting the optimal movement field at each training step. We treat the distributional dependencies as fixed targets during backpropagation, avoiding the need to differentiate through complex sample interactions. This approach connects to classical density estimation methods while providing a principled framework for training modern neural architectures. Flow Matching achieves competitive sample quality across multiple modalities while requiring only single forward passes at inference time, offering a computationally efficient alternative to iterative generative models without sacrificing modeling capacity or training stability.
