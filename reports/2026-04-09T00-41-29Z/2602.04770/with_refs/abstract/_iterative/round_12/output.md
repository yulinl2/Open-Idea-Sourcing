# Flow Matching: Single-Step Generative Modeling Through Distributional Dynamics

## Abstract

Current generative models face a fundamental trade-off between sample quality and computational efficiency, with high-quality approaches requiring iterative refinement during inference. We introduce **Flow Matching**, a novel training paradigm that learns to generate samples in a single forward pass by directly optimizing distributional transformations during training rather than inference. Our approach formulates generative modeling as learning a vector field that governs how samples move from a simple prior distribution toward the target data distribution. During each training iteration, we compute explicit movement vectors for generated samples based on their pairwise relationships with real data samples and other generated samples, creating a balanced field of attractive and repulsive forces. The key insight is that samples are simultaneously attracted toward data points while being repelled from other generated samples, naturally preventing mode collapse. Unlike adversarial methods, Flow Matching requires no discriminator and avoids the distributional dependency problem by treating computed movements as fixed targets during backpropagation. The vector field reaches equilibrium when the generated distribution aligns with the data distribution, at which point sample movement naturally ceases. Our method connects to classical kernel-based density estimation while leveraging modern neural network capacity, enabling the iterative nature of training to replace the iterative nature of inference. Flow Matching scales effectively to high-dimensional data, supports flexible conditional generation, and works across multiple modalities. The approach provides a principled framework where the network learns to transform its input distribution through discrete training updates, ultimately producing high-quality samples in a single forward pass without sacrificing the modeling power that makes iterative methods successful.
