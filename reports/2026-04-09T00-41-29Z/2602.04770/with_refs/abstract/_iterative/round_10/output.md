# Flow Matching: Single-Step Generative Modeling via Direct Distribution Transformation

## Abstract

Current generative models face a fundamental trade-off between sample quality and computational efficiency, with most high-quality approaches requiring iterative refinement during inference. We propose **Flow Matching**, a novel training paradigm that learns to generate high-quality samples in a single forward pass by directly optimizing how the network transforms its input distribution toward the target data distribution. Our key insight is to leverage the iterative nature of training itself rather than inference: during each training step, we compute explicit movement vectors that simultaneously attract generated samples toward real data points while repelling them from other generated samples. This creates a vector field that governs distributional evolution, where the field strength is determined by weighted pairwise interactions between samples. Specifically, we define the movement field as $v_t(x) = \mathbb{E}_{x_d \sim p_{\text{data}}}[w_+(x, x_d)(x_d - x)] - \mathbb{E}_{x_g \sim p_t}[w_-(x, x_g)(x_g - x)]$, where $w_+$ and $w_-$ are kernel-based weighting functions that ensure anti-symmetric behavior and convergence to equilibrium when distributions align. Unlike adversarial approaches, our method requires no discriminator and avoids the distributional dependency problem by treating the current generated distribution as a fixed target during backpropagation. The training objective directly minimizes the divergence between consecutive distributional iterates, ensuring that sample movement naturally diminishes as the generated distribution approaches the target. Our approach connects to classical kernel-based density estimation while providing a principled framework for learning complex distribution mappings. Flow Matching enables single-step generation with quality comparable to iterative methods, supports flexible conditioning, and scales effectively across modalities. The method provides a new perspective on generative modeling where the evolution happens during training through discrete distributional updates rather than continuous dynamics at inference time.
