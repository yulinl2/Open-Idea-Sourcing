# Flow Matching: Single-Step Generative Modeling via Distributional Movement Fields

## Abstract

Current generative models face a fundamental trade-off between sample quality and computational efficiency, with state-of-the-art methods requiring iterative refinement procedures that perform multiple network evaluations during inference. We propose Flow Matching, a novel training paradigm that learns to generate high-quality samples in a single forward pass by directly optimizing how the generated distribution moves toward the data distribution during training. Our approach formulates generation as learning a vector field that governs sample movement, where each training iteration explicitly moves generated samples closer to real data through discrete distributional updates. Specifically, we define a movement field F(x,P_g,P_d) that combines attractive forces from data samples and repulsive forces from other generated samples, creating an anti-symmetric field that naturally reaches equilibrium when distributions align. The key insight is to leverage the iterative nature of training itself rather than requiring iteration at inference time. During each training step, we compute explicit movement vectors for generated samples based on weighted pairwise interactions: samples are simultaneously attracted to nearby data points and repelled from other generated samples, with movement magnitudes determined by local density estimates. This creates a principled objective that avoids adversarial training while ensuring the learned transformation maps the input noise distribution to the target data distribution. The movement field is designed to be zero at equilibrium, providing theoretical guarantees for convergence. Unlike existing approaches that rely on discriminative signals or continuous dynamics, Flow Matching directly optimizes the discrete evolution of the output distribution through the training process. This enables single-step generation with quality comparable to iterative methods while maintaining training stability and supporting flexible conditioning across different data modalities. The approach connects to classical kernel-based density estimation methods while providing a modern neural framework for efficient generative modeling.
