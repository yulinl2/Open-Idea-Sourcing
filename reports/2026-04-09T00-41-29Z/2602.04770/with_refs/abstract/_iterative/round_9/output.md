# Flow Matching: Single-Step Generative Modeling via Optimal Transport

## Abstract

Generative modeling faces a fundamental trade-off between sample quality and computational efficiency, as most high-quality approaches require iterative refinement during inference. We propose **Flow Matching**, a novel training paradigm that learns to generate high-quality samples in a single forward pass by directly optimizing the transport between noise and data distributions. Our approach formulates generation as learning a vector field that governs how samples should move during training iterations, where each batch explicitly pushes the generated distribution closer to the target through discrete optimal transport steps. Unlike adversarial or variational methods, Flow Matching uses pairwise interactions between generated and real samples to compute movement vectors based on kernel-weighted similarities, creating an anti-symmetric flow field that naturally defines equilibrium when distributions align. The key insight is to leverage the iterative nature of training itself—rather than inference—to evolve distributions, while preventing direct backpropagation through distributional dependencies by treating transport computations as fixed targets. During training, we compute explicit displacement vectors for each generated sample based on its relationships to real samples, with movements governed by a learned field that depends on both current and target distributions. This connects to classical optimal transport theory while enabling end-to-end learning of complex, high-dimensional mappings. Flow Matching achieves single-step generation without sacrificing modeling capacity, supports flexible conditional generation across modalities, and provides a principled framework that converges reliably without mode collapse. Our approach fundamentally reimagines generative modeling by moving the iterative refinement process from inference time to training time, enabling efficient high-quality generation through learned optimal transport.
