# Flow Matching: Training Generative Models via Sample Transport

## Abstract

Current generative modeling approaches face a fundamental trade-off between generation quality and computational efficiency. While discriminative models map individual samples to labels, generative models must learn complex mappings between entire distributions, often requiring iterative procedures at inference time that perform multiple network evaluations. We propose **Flow Matching**, a novel training paradigm that learns to generate high-quality samples in a single forward pass by leveraging the iterative nature of training itself rather than inference. Our approach formulates generative modeling as learning a vector field that governs how samples move from a simple prior distribution toward the target data distribution. During each training iteration, we compute explicit movement vectors for generated samples based on their pairwise relationships with real data samples, using kernel-weighted interactions that are anti-symmetric with respect to swapping the two distributions. Crucially, the network learns to predict these discrete sample movements without directly backpropagating through the distributional dependencies, treating the movement targets as fixed during gradient computation. This creates an equilibrium condition where sample transport naturally stops when the generated and data distributions align. Unlike adversarial approaches, Flow Matching requires no discriminator and avoids mode collapse by directly encoding attractive and repulsive forces between samples. The method scales effectively to high-resolution data, supports flexible conditional generation, and provides a principled training objective that converges reliably. Our approach connects classical density estimation techniques with modern neural architectures, offering a computationally efficient alternative to iterative generative models while maintaining their modeling capacity through the learned transport dynamics.
