# Single-Step Distribution Mapping via Optimal Transport Flows

## Abstract

Current generative modeling approaches face a fundamental trade-off between sample quality and computational efficiency. While iterative methods like diffusion models achieve high-quality generation through multi-step refinement processes, they require numerous forward passes at inference time, creating significant computational overhead. Conversely, single-step methods often struggle to learn complex distribution mappings directly, resulting in lower sample quality or training instability. We propose **Optimal Transport Flow Networks (OTFlow)**, a novel generative modeling framework that learns to map from a simple prior distribution to complex data distributions in a single forward pass by leveraging principles from optimal transport theory.

Our approach formulates generative modeling as learning the optimal transport map between the prior and data distributions, which we parameterize using neural networks with specialized architectural constraints that ensure the learned mapping preserves mass and minimizes transport cost. The key insight is that optimal transport provides a principled way to decompose complex distribution mappings into simpler, learnable components while maintaining theoretical guarantees about the quality of the learned transformation. We introduce a novel training objective that combines Wasserstein distance minimization with a regularization term that encourages smooth, invertible mappings.

OTFlow networks are trained using a hybrid approach that alternates between estimating the optimal transport plan via Sinkhorn iterations and updating network parameters to better approximate this plan. This avoids the adversarial training dynamics that plague GANs while providing more direct optimization than maximum likelihood approaches. The method naturally handles conditional generation by learning transport maps conditioned on auxiliary information, and scales efficiently to high-dimensional data through hierarchical transport decompositions.

Our framework offers several key advantages: (1) single-step generation with competitive sample quality, (2) stable training without mode collapse, (3) theoretical grounding in optimal transport theory, (4) natural extension to conditional and controllable generation, and (5) computational efficiency at inference time. We demonstrate that OTFlow achieves comparable sample quality to multi-step diffusion models while requiring only a single forward pass, representing a significant advance toward efficient, high-quality generative modeling across diverse data modalities.
