# Distribution Evolution Networks: Single-Pass Generative Modeling via Training-Time Distribution Matching

## Abstract

Current generative models face a fundamental trade-off between sample quality and computational efficiency, with high-quality methods requiring expensive iterative refinement at inference time. We introduce Distribution Evolution Networks (DENs), a novel generative modeling framework that achieves single-pass generation by leveraging the iterative nature of neural network training itself. Our key insight is that the evolution of a model's generated distribution during training can be viewed as a trajectory through distribution space, and we can define an equilibrium condition where this trajectory converges to the target data distribution.

DENs employ a dual-network architecture consisting of a generator network and a distribution matcher network. During training, the generator produces samples that evolve with the changing network parameters, while the distribution matcher learns to predict when the generated distribution has reached equilibrium with the data distribution. We introduce a novel training objective based on distribution distance minimization that does not require adversarial optimization, instead using direct interactions between generated and real samples through optimal transport-inspired matching.

The equilibrium condition is defined through a learnable stopping criterion that measures distributional convergence via weighted sample comparisons, similar to conformal prediction techniques but adapted for generative modeling. This approach naturally prevents mode collapse by ensuring the generated distribution maintains sufficient diversity throughout the training trajectory. Unlike iterative refinement methods, DENs require only a single forward pass at inference time, as the trained generator has internalized the distribution mapping learned during the evolutionary training process.

Our framework addresses the core challenge of mapping between probability distributions by transforming the problem from inference-time iteration to training-time distribution evolution. The resulting model achieves competitive sample quality while maintaining the computational efficiency of single-pass generation, offering a principled alternative to both adversarial training and multi-step refinement procedures. This work establishes a new paradigm for generative modeling that exploits the natural iterative dynamics of neural network optimization to learn complex distribution mappings.
