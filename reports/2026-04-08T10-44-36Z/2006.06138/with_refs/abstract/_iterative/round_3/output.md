# Conformal Prediction Intervals for Individual Treatment Effects with Covariate Shift Correction

## Abstract

Understanding heterogeneous treatment effects is crucial for personalized decision-making in medicine and policy, yet existing methods for quantifying uncertainty around individual treatment effects suffer from fundamental limitations. While recent advances in conformal prediction have shown promise for uncertainty quantification in causal inference, current approaches fail to address a critical challenge: the distribution of covariates typically differs between treated and control groups, creating covariate shift that invalidates standard prediction intervals for counterfactual outcomes. We propose a novel doubly-robust conformal prediction framework that explicitly corrects for covariate shift when constructing prediction intervals for individual treatment effects. Our method combines importance weighting with conformal prediction to handle distributional differences between treatment groups, while maintaining exact finite-sample coverage guarantees under randomized treatment assignment. The key innovation is a weighted conformal score that rebalances the covariate distributions when computing quantiles for counterfactual predictions. We establish that our intervals achieve valid coverage when either the outcome model or the propensity score model is correctly specified, providing robustness against model misspecification. The approach works for both in-sample subjects and new individuals from potentially different target populations. Unlike existing methods that rely on asymptotic approximations or strong parametric assumptions, our framework provides distribution-free guarantees in finite samples while accommodating flexible machine learning algorithms for both outcome and treatment assignment modeling. This addresses a critical gap in the causal inference literature by enabling reliable uncertainty quantification for personalized treatment decisions in realistic settings where treatment groups exhibit covariate imbalance.
