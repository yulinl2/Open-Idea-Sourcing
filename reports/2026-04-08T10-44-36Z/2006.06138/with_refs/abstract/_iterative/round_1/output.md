# Bayesian Conformal Prediction for Heterogeneous Treatment Effect Uncertainty

## Abstract

Personalized treatment decisions require understanding not just the expected effect for each individual, but also the uncertainty around those predictions. While recent advances in causal machine learning have enabled flexible estimation of conditional average treatment effects (CATE), existing approaches provide poor uncertainty quantification, particularly for individual-level predictions where we never observe both potential outcomes. Current conformal prediction methods for treatment effects either require strong distributional assumptions or produce overly conservative intervals that limit practical utility. We propose a novel Bayesian conformal framework that combines the distribution-free finite-sample guarantees of conformal prediction with principled Bayesian uncertainty quantification over both the regression functions and error distributions. Our approach constructs prediction intervals for individual treatment effects by: (1) using variational Bayesian neural networks to model outcome surfaces under treatment and control while quantifying epistemic uncertainty, (2) employing a novel split-conformal procedure that accounts for the fundamental asymmetry in treatment assignment, and (3) introducing correlation-adaptive interval combination that leverages posterior samples to estimate the dependence structure between potential outcome errors without requiring parametric assumptions. The method provides finite-sample coverage guarantees while adapting interval width to both aleatoric uncertainty (inherent outcome variability) and epistemic uncertainty (model uncertainty from finite samples). Unlike existing approaches, our intervals naturally shrink in regions where the model is confident while expanding where treatment effect heterogeneity is high or data is sparse. The framework handles both randomized experiments and observational studies under standard causal assumptions, extends to covariate shift between study and target populations, and provides computationally efficient posterior sampling for scalable uncertainty quantification in high-dimensional settings.
