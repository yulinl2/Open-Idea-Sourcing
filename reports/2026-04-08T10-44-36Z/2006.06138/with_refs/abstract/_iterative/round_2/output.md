# Conformal Prediction Intervals for Heterogeneous Treatment Effects Under Covariate Shift

## Abstract

Understanding how treatments affect individuals differently is crucial for personalized medicine and policy-making, yet existing methods for quantifying uncertainty in heterogeneous treatment effects suffer from poor finite-sample guarantees and fail to address covariate shift between treatment groups. We propose a novel conformal prediction framework that constructs distribution-free prediction intervals for individual treatment effects while explicitly handling the fundamental challenge that treated and control populations have different covariate distributions, even in randomized experiments with non-constant propensity scores.

Our approach introduces **covariate-shift-aware conformal prediction** that reweights conformal scores using estimated propensity scores to account for distributional differences between treatment arms. We develop three complementary procedures: (1) a conservative finite-sample method requiring only exchangeability within treatment groups, (2) an improved procedure under conditional independence of potential outcome errors, and (3) an asymptotically optimal method leveraging consistency assumptions and Gaussian error structure. Critically, our framework maintains exact coverage guarantees when either the outcome model or the propensity score model is correctly specified, providing robustness against model misspecification through a doubly-robust property.

The key innovation lies in constructing separate conformal prediction intervals for each treatment arm using reweighted nonconformity scores that account for covariate shift, then combining these via careful probability bounds to ensure valid coverage for the unobservable individual treatment effect. Our approach works for both in-sample subjects and new individuals from potentially different target populations, enabling reliable uncertainty quantification for treatment effect heterogeneity without strong parametric assumptions. Extensive simulations demonstrate that our method achieves nominal coverage while producing substantially narrower intervals than existing approaches, particularly when leveraging flexible machine learning algorithms for outcome prediction. This framework addresses a critical gap in causal inference by providing the first distribution-free, finite-sample valid prediction intervals for individual treatment effects under realistic covariate shift conditions.
