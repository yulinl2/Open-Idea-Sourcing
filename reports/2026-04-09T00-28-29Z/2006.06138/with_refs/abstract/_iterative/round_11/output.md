# Conformal Counterfactual Prediction: Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification for Individual Treatment Effects

Understanding how treatments affect individuals differently is crucial for personalized decision-making in medicine, policy, and social sciences. While methods for estimating conditional average treatment effects (CATE) have advanced significantly, reliable uncertainty quantification for individual-level effects remains challenging. Standard approaches rely on asymptotic approximations or strong parametric assumptions that may not hold in practice. A fundamental obstacle is that we never observe both potential outcomes for any individual, creating a missing data problem that standard prediction methods cannot directly address. Additionally, the covariate distributions between treated and control groups typically differ even in randomized experiments, creating covariate shift that complicates counterfactual prediction.

We propose **conformal counterfactual prediction**, a distribution-free framework that provides finite-sample prediction intervals for individual treatment effects without parametric assumptions. Our approach addresses the covariate shift problem by extending weighted conformal prediction to the causal setting. Specifically, we construct prediction intervals for counterfactual outcomes by reweighting conformal scores using inverse propensity weights, where observations are weighted by the inverse probability of receiving their observed treatment. This reweighting ensures that when predicting the counterfactual outcome for a treated individual, we appropriately emphasize control group observations that are representative of the treated population's covariate distribution.

Our method provides exact finite-sample coverage guarantees for randomized experiments where propensity scores are known by design. The procedure maintains validity under model misspecification—either the outcome model or the propensity score model can be wrong, but not necessarily both. We establish that our weighted conformal intervals achieve the target coverage rate $1-\alpha$ exactly, without requiring large-sample approximations. For individual treatment effect intervals, we combine counterfactual prediction intervals using a union bound approach that accounts for the dependence structure between potential outcomes.

The key technical innovation is showing that weighted exchangeability holds for the reweighted nonconformity scores in the causal setting, enabling valid conformal inference despite covariate shift. This provides a principled solution to uncertainty quantification for personalized treatment decisions, with immediate applications to precision medicine and policy evaluation where reliable individual-level predictions are essential for decision-making.
