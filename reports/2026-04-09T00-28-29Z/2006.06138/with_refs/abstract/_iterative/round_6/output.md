**Conformal Prediction Intervals for Heterogeneous Treatment Effects Under Covariate Shift**

Understanding how treatments affect individuals differently is crucial for personalized decision-making in medicine, policy, and social sciences. While recent advances focus on estimating conditional average treatment effects (CATE) using flexible machine learning methods, these approaches provide limited uncertainty quantification—a critical gap in high-stakes applications where understanding prediction reliability is essential. The fundamental challenge is that we never observe both potential outcomes for any individual, and existing methods typically assume the covariate distributions of treated and control groups are identical, which rarely holds even in randomized experiments with non-constant propensity scores.

We propose a novel framework that extends conformal prediction to construct distribution-free prediction intervals for individual treatment effects under covariate shift. Our approach addresses the key challenge that standard conformal methods fail when the covariate distribution differs between treatment groups by introducing a doubly-weighted conformal procedure. For each individual, we construct separate weighted conformal intervals for their potential outcomes under treatment and control, where weights account for both the treatment assignment mechanism (propensity scores) and the covariate shift between the study population and target population of interest. These intervals are then combined using Bonferroni-type corrections to yield valid prediction intervals for the individual treatment effect.

Our method provides exact finite-sample coverage guarantees in randomized experiments where propensity scores are known by design, requiring only that either the outcome model or the propensity model (but not necessarily both) is correctly specified. Unlike existing approaches that rely on asymptotic approximations or strong parametric assumptions, our intervals maintain validity under arbitrary data distributions and flexible machine learning algorithms. The framework naturally handles the sequential inference problem by first constructing valid counterfactual intervals for study participants before extending to new populations, and accommodates settings where the study population differs from the target population through appropriate reweighting schemes. This provides practitioners with reliable, assumption-light uncertainty quantification for personalized treatment decisions.
