# Conformal Prediction Intervals for Individual Treatment Effects Under Covariate Shift

## Abstract

Understanding heterogeneous treatment effects is crucial for personalized decision-making, yet existing methods for quantifying uncertainty about individual-level causal effects face fundamental challenges. Standard approaches focus on estimating conditional average treatment effects but provide poor uncertainty quantification, while the covariate distributions between treated and control groups typically differ even in randomized experiments, creating a covariate shift problem that invalidates standard prediction methods for counterfactual outcomes. We propose a novel extension of conformal prediction that addresses both challenges simultaneously by constructing distribution-free prediction intervals for individual treatment effects that account for treatment assignment mechanisms. Our method leverages weighted conformal prediction to handle covariate shift between treatment groups, using importance weights derived from known or estimated propensity scores to reweight nonconformity scores appropriately. We develop three variants: a conservative finite-sample procedure requiring only exchangeability assumptions, an improved version under conditional independence of potential outcome errors, and an asymptotic procedure for consistent estimators under normality assumptions. The key insight is to construct separate weighted conformal intervals for each potential outcome, then combine them using careful probability bounds to ensure valid coverage for the unobservable individual treatment effect. Our approach provides exact finite-sample coverage guarantees in randomized experiments where propensity scores are known by design, maintains validity when either the outcome model or propensity model is misspecified (but not both), and naturally handles the fundamental problem that we never observe both potential outcomes for any individual. Unlike existing methods that rely on asymptotic approximations or strong parametric assumptions, our framework delivers reliable uncertainty quantification for individual-level causal effects while properly accounting for the covariate shift inherent in causal inference problems.
