# Conformal Prediction Intervals for Individual Treatment Effects Under Covariate Shift

## Abstract

Understanding heterogeneous treatment effects is crucial for personalized decision-making, yet existing methods for quantifying uncertainty about individual-level causal effects face fundamental challenges. Current approaches either rely on asymptotic approximations that may fail in finite samples or ignore the covariate shift problem that arises when the distribution of covariates differs between treatment and control groups. We propose a novel extension of conformal prediction that addresses both challenges simultaneously by developing distribution-free prediction intervals for individual treatment effects that remain valid under covariate shift. Our method combines weighted conformal prediction with a doubly-robust approach to handle the fundamental problem of causal inference—that we never observe both potential outcomes for any individual. Specifically, we construct separate weighted conformal intervals for each potential outcome using importance weights derived from the known propensity score, then combine these intervals using a min-max approach that accounts for the correlation structure between potential outcomes. The resulting intervals achieve exact finite-sample coverage guarantees in randomized experiments without requiring parametric assumptions about the outcome models or error distributions. Our approach maintains validity even when either the outcome model or the propensity score model is misspecified (but not both), making it robust to model misspecification. Unlike existing methods that focus primarily on conditional average treatment effects, our intervals directly quantify uncertainty about individual-level effects while properly handling the covariate imbalance inherent in causal inference problems. The method extends naturally to settings where the target population differs from the study population, enabling reliable uncertainty quantification for treatment effect predictions on new individuals. This work bridges the gap between the conformal prediction literature and causal inference, providing practitioners with a principled tool for personalized treatment decisions with rigorous uncertainty quantification.
