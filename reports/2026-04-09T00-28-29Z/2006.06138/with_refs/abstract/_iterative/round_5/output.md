# Weighted Conformal Prediction for Individual Treatment Effects

Understanding how treatments affect individuals differently is crucial for personalized medicine and policy-making, yet existing methods for estimating conditional average treatment effects (CATE) provide limited uncertainty quantification. While recent work has explored conformal prediction for individual treatment effects, these approaches fail to address a fundamental challenge: the distribution of covariates typically differs between treated and control groups, creating covariate shift that invalidates standard prediction methods when constructing intervals for counterfactual outcomes.

We propose a weighted conformal prediction framework that provides distribution-free uncertainty quantification for individual treatment effects while properly handling covariate shift between treatment groups. Our approach leverages the known treatment assignment mechanism (propensity scores) to reweight observations when constructing prediction intervals for counterfactual outcomes. Specifically, we extend weighted conformal prediction to the causal setting by: (1) constructing separate weighted conformal intervals for potential outcomes under treatment and control, where weights are derived from inverse propensity score ratios; (2) combining these intervals using Bonferroni correction or conditional independence assumptions to obtain valid intervals for the individual treatment effect τ(X) = Y(1) - Y(0).

Our method achieves exact finite-sample coverage guarantees for randomized experiments where propensity scores are known by design, without requiring parametric modeling assumptions about the outcome generating process. The approach maintains validity under model misspecification of either the outcome model or the propensity score model (but not both simultaneously). Unlike existing approaches that focus on population-level inference, we first solve the fundamental problem of constructing reliable intervals for counterfactual outcomes of subjects within the study population.

The key technical innovation lies in properly weighting the empirical distribution of nonconformity scores to account for the fact that treated and control subjects represent different populations when making counterfactual predictions. This addresses the covariate shift problem inherent in causal inference while preserving the distribution-free guarantees of conformal prediction. Our framework provides practitioners with reliable uncertainty quantification for individual-level treatment decisions, particularly valuable in high-stakes applications where understanding the range of possible treatment effects is as important as point estimates.
