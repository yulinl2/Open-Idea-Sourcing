# Weighted Conformal Prediction for Individual Treatment Effect Quantification

Understanding how treatments affect individuals differently is crucial for personalized medicine and evidence-based decision making, yet existing methods for estimating conditional average treatment effects provide limited uncertainty quantification. While recent advances have adapted conformal prediction to handle covariate shift in standard prediction problems, the causal inference setting presents unique challenges: we never observe both potential outcomes for any individual, and the distribution of covariates typically differs between treatment groups even in randomized experiments, creating fundamental obstacles for reliable uncertainty quantification about counterfactual outcomes.

We propose a novel weighted conformal prediction framework specifically designed for constructing prediction intervals around individual treatment effects. Our approach addresses the covariate shift problem inherent in causal inference by leveraging the known propensity scores in randomized experiments to appropriately reweight conformal scores. Specifically, we construct separate prediction intervals for each potential outcome using weighted conformal prediction, where observations are reweighted by the inverse of their treatment assignment probabilities. These intervals are then combined using a conservative union bound to produce valid prediction intervals for the unobserved individual treatment effect.

Our method provides several key advantages: (1) it achieves exact finite-sample coverage guarantees in randomized experiments without requiring asymptotic approximations or parametric assumptions about the outcome distribution; (2) it maintains validity under misspecification of either the outcome model or the propensity score model, but not both simultaneously; (3) it naturally handles the covariate shift between treatment and control groups by incorporating the experimental design through appropriate reweighting; and (4) it works with any base prediction algorithm, from linear regression to complex machine learning methods.

The theoretical foundation rests on extending weighted exchangeability concepts to the causal setting, where we show that appropriately reweighted conformal scores maintain their distribution-free coverage properties even when treatment assignment creates covariate imbalance. We demonstrate that our intervals achieve the desired coverage while remaining computationally efficient through a split conformal approach that avoids refitting models for each potential outcome value.

This work provides the first distribution-free method for uncertainty quantification of individual treatment effects that explicitly accounts for the experimental design and maintains exact coverage guarantees in finite samples, enabling more reliable personalized decision-making in sensitive applications.
