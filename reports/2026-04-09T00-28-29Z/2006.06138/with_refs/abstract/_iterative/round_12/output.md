# Weighted Conformal Prediction for Counterfactual Inference

Understanding how treatments affect individuals differently is crucial for personalized medicine, policy evaluation, and other decision-making domains. While researchers have developed sophisticated machine learning methods for estimating conditional average treatment effects (CATE), these approaches typically provide poor uncertainty quantification—a critical limitation when making high-stakes decisions about individual-level interventions. Existing theoretical guarantees rely on asymptotic approximations or strong distributional assumptions that may not hold in practice, and fundamentally, we face the challenge that we never observe both potential outcomes for any individual.

We propose a novel approach that adapts conformal prediction to the causal inference setting by addressing the inherent covariate shift between treatment and control groups. Our method constructs distribution-free prediction intervals for individual counterfactual outcomes by reweighting conformal scores according to the inverse propensity score (treatment assignment probability). Specifically, we develop weighted conformal prediction intervals that achieve exact finite-sample coverage guarantees for counterfactual predictions in randomized experiments, where propensity scores are known by design.

Our approach works by first constructing separate conformal prediction intervals for outcomes under each treatment condition, using weighted quantiles that account for the covariate distribution differences between groups. We then combine these intervals to produce prediction intervals for individual treatment effects. The method maintains validity under model misspecification—requiring only that either the outcome model or the propensity score model (but not necessarily both) be correctly specified. This provides a crucial robustness property for real-world applications where perfect model specification is unlikely.

The key insight is that by appropriately reweighting the empirical distribution of nonconformity scores according to the likelihood ratio between treatment and control covariate distributions, we can restore the exchangeability property required for conformal prediction. This enables us to provide finite-sample coverage guarantees without parametric assumptions about the outcome distribution or the functional form relating covariates to outcomes. Our intervals are particularly valuable for the initial step of quantifying uncertainty about counterfactual outcomes for subjects already in the study, before attempting to generalize to new populations—a foundational problem that has received insufficient attention in the causal inference literature.
