# Conformal Prediction Intervals for Individual Causal Effects

Understanding how treatments affect individuals differently is crucial for personalized decision-making, yet existing methods for estimating conditional average treatment effects (CATE) provide poor uncertainty quantification. While machine learning algorithms can estimate heterogeneous treatment effects, they typically produce point estimates without reliable confidence intervals, limiting their utility in high-stakes applications where assessing individual-level risk is essential. The fundamental challenge stems from the fact that only one potential outcome is observed per individual, combined with two sources of uncertainty: the inherent variability in individual responses and finite-sample estimation error.

We propose **Causal Conformal Prediction (CCP)**, a distribution-free framework that constructs valid prediction intervals for individual treatment effects without parametric assumptions. Our approach leverages the potential outcomes framework by treating the unobserved counterfactual as a missing data problem amenable to conformal inference. For each individual, we define nonconformity scores based on residuals from separate outcome models fitted on treated and control units, then use a novel weighting scheme that accounts for propensity score differences when constructing prediction intervals. The key insight is that by appropriately reweighting the empirical distribution of nonconformity scores according to covariate similarity and treatment assignment probabilities, we can achieve finite-sample coverage guarantees even when CATE models are misspecified.

Our method extends naturally to handle covariate shift between study and target populations by incorporating likelihood ratio weights, enabling valid inference when deploying treatment effect estimates to new populations. We establish that CCP achieves marginal coverage of at least 1-α in finite samples under standard causal assumptions, while maintaining computational efficiency through a split conformal approach that separates model fitting from interval construction. This provides practitioners with a principled tool for quantifying uncertainty in personalized treatment recommendations.
